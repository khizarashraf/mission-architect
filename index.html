A simplified learning model based on the four mission segments in NASA's mission design overview: launch, space, ground and user. The numbers are invented.

Welcome, mission engineer

Your job: design a space mission, spend a limited budget, and make sure it survives the trip. You do not need to know any space science. The game teaches you as you play.

Pick a place to explore, build a spacecraft, press launch, and find out if it works. If it fails, the game tells you exactly why.

How a real mission is built

  • Launch: a rocket carries your spacecraft off Earth.
  • Space: the spacecraft has instruments (the science tools) and a power system.
  • Ground: an antenna sends your results home to Earth.
  • Users: scientists use the data. That is your score.

Four words to know

  • Payload capacity: how much weight a rocket can lift. Heavier spacecraft need bigger, costlier rockets.
  • Power: electricity for your instruments. Solar panels make less far from the Sun.
  • Data rate: how much information the antenna can send home, like internet speed. Far away means slower.
  • Safety margin: spare power or budget left over, in case something goes wrong.
